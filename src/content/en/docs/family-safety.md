Social Home is built for households, and households include
children. **Family safety** lets a household admin give a child
an account with sensible guardrails baked in — without turning
the place into a surveillance system.

## Protected accounts

A household admin can mark a member as a **protected minor**, set
their **declared age**, and assign one or more **guardians**.
That declared age then quietly shapes what the account can reach:

- **Age-gated spaces** — a protected minor can't join a space
  whose minimum age is higher than their declared age. The gate
  is enforced everywhere a member gets seated, including across
  paired households, and it travels with the space when it
  federates — so a remote space can't sneak past it.
- **Age-restricted apps** — apps an admin has put behind an age
  gate won't open for an underage account.
- **DMs stay close to home** — a protected minor's direct
  messages are limited to households you've directly paired with,
  not the wider federation.

## The guardian view

A guardian gets a **read-only** overview of the people and places
in their child's world — the spaces they're in, who they're
talking to, their DM contacts, and anyone they've blocked. It's a
window for a parent to stay aware, not a set of remote controls,
and it never reaches into message contents.

## Age gates are a space setting too

Any space — not just ones with children in mind — can set a
**minimum age** and a target audience. Admins set it once; it's
enforced on every join, local or federated, and an older peer
that doesn't understand the field simply treats the space as
having no restriction. See [Global spaces](/docs/global-spaces/)
for how a relay carries an age policy into a public directory.
