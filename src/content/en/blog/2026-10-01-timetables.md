It's 07:53 on a Thursday. Emma's first lesson was cancelled, Leo's
reading starts at daycare in seven minutes, the dentist is at 09:15,
and somewhere in the house the swimming bag still needs to be found.
A paper timetable on the fridge only covers half of that.

Timetables in Social Home cover the rest. Each child gets a weekly
plan that knows about school holidays and about this week's changes.
Today's lessons are merged with the household calendar on your home
screen. And when a class-parents space keeps the timetable, every
household in the class sees the same plan, from its own server.

_All screens below show sample data: three fictional children, Emma
(class 4b), Leo (daycare and music school) and Mia. The sample
timetable was entered with German subject names. The app itself
follows your language setting._

## Three ways to read a week

Find it under **Calendar › Timetable**. The grid picks its layout for
you. When every day shares the same slot times, you get the classic
**periods** layout: numbered rows, a colour per subject, and double
lessons merged into one block. When days differ (an early music
lesson on Tuesday, a long swimming block on Wednesday), it switches
to a **timeline** where block height follows the clock. Empty
stretches are compressed, so the afternoon doesn't eat the screen.

For kids who can't read yet there is a **picture view**: big icons
with a small caption, good for a tablet on the kitchen wall. On a
phone the week becomes a day view with chips to switch days.

## Setting it up in minutes

Nobody wants to type thirty lessons into thirty dialogs. A new
timetable starts from a **school day** template. The **day builder**
then asks three questions (when does the first lesson start, how long
is a lesson, how long are the gaps) and works out every time from
there. Insert an early lesson with “+ Add before” and the rest of the
day moves to match.

Then switch on **brush mode**: pick a subject and click or drag
across the slots it covers. A break band applies to every day, so you
fix the 09:35 break once, not five times.

## School weeks and holidays

A timetable belongs to one continuous school year, September to July.
The school-weeks picker lists every week as a chip. Mark the holiday
weeks once and the plan goes quiet during them. Holiday weeks are
striped and carry a 🏖, so you can tell them apart without relying on
colour. If school runs anyway, one click on “Activate this week”
brings the plan back.

## When this week is different

Teachers get sick, rooms move and choir rehearsals get added. Switch
to **This week** and change a single lesson for just this week:
cancel it, change the subject, room or time, or add an extra lesson.
Everything you don't touch follows the regular plan. Next Monday the
changes are gone on their own, and “Clear all” resets the week early
if you need it.

## Today, on your home screen

This is where the timetable earns its place in a household OS. The
**Today** card on your welcome page merges every child's lessons for
today with today's events from the [calendar](/docs/calendar/). It
shows **Now** and **Next** at the top, a red now line through the
list, and a ⚠ wherever an event overlaps a lesson, like the dentist
appointment during English. With several children, a “Show:” filter
narrows the card to one of them. Rows that are already over fold
away, and once the last lesson ends the card simply says
“School's out”.

## Shared with the class

Social Home is also a federated social network, and timetables use
that. A class-parents [space](/docs/global-spaces/) can keep **one**
timetable for the whole class. The space admins get the full editor.
Every member household gets a read-only view (with room, teacher and
notes) and a **🏠 Show on my Home** toggle that pins it to their own
Today card.

The plan travels the way everything in a space does: household to
household, between [paired servers](/docs/federation/). When the
class rep moves PE to Friday, every pinned household sees the change.
Nobody has to send a group message about it, and no central service
holds a copy of the class schedule. The Timetable feature is off by
default for each space, so a space admin switches it on in the
space's settings.

## Small things that came along

Two settings came out of this work and apply everywhere. You can now
choose the **first day of the week**, and new timetables start their
week on it. The **language picker** remembers
your choice across reloads and devices and works with the keyboard.

## What's next

Timetables made it clear how much of a household's day fits on one
glanceable screen. So the next feature is a **home dashboard**: a
calm overview of the day for the whole household. It will also work
on **ESPHome displays**, so a small screen by the front door or in
the kitchen can show who has what today without anyone unlocking a
phone. It's still being built, and we'll write about it here when
it's ready to try.
