Spaces are where Social Home meets the world outside your front door:
the family group with Grandma, the choir, the neighbours, the parents'
council. They were also the last big part of the app still on the old
look: tilted paper cards, up to ten tabs in a row, and a phone that
made you scroll past a cover and the full About text before you could
write a post.

So we went through every screen under Spaces in three review rounds:
an audit of the app as it was, layout experiments, and a fresh design
review against the redesigned Connections and Friends pages. The
result ships in **Social Home 2026.10.20**: calmer pages, fewer taps,
and a new kind of public space, the **local community**.

_All screens below show a sample household: the Bergers, with Anna,
Jonas, Mia, Leo and Grandma Rosa, around Zürich._

## One Spaces page

Spaces and Browse used to be two pages with different card styles.
Now there is one: a pending invite on top (with the space's name, its
picture and who invited you), then **Yours**, then the spaces you
**Follow**, then **Discover** to find more, and your archive folded
away at the bottom. Every row says how many people are in the space,
when it was last active, and how many posts are new since you last
looked.

Discover has three tabs: **Your household**, **Paired households**
and **Global**. Household spaces now work the way the app always
described them: everyone at home is a member, and an open household
space you haven't joined yet shows up in Discover. Spaces that need an
invite never appear there.

## A space opens on its people

A space now opens on a compact home: the cover, the name, the faces of
its members, two lines of About with **Read more**, the quick links,
and the composer. On a phone the composer used to start 1,133 pixels
down, more than a full screen of scrolling. Now it starts at 465.

## Tabs that fit your screen

A space can have up to ten sections: Feed, Chat, Calendar, Tasks,
Pages, Gallery, Stickies, Bazaar, Map and Timetable. On a phone you
used to see two of them. Now the tab row shows as many as fit, always
in the same order, and the rest move under **More**. A desktop shows
them all.

More also holds **Members**, **Moderation** with its count, and **your
notifications** for that space. There is one bell for the whole app
now, in the top bar, instead of a second one inside each space.

Each section has its own address, so Refresh and Back keep you where
you were, and you can send someone a link straight to a space's
calendar.

## Settings on one page

A space's settings were spread over several tabs, and the General tab
alone was about 4,800 pixels long with a single Save button near the
bottom. Now everything is on one page, with a list of sections on the
side (or chips on top, on a phone) to jump between them: Basics, Who
can find it, Members & joining, Sections, Your notifications,
Location & zones, Moderation & age, Bots, and Archive or delete.

Only **Basics** (the name, the description, About, quick links and the
theme) waits for **Save**, and the save bar only appears once you've
changed something. Everything else saves the moment you flip it.

An owner can now **hand a space over** to someone else in the
household, and a household admin can give a space a new owner when its
owner has left.

## A new space asks what it's for

Create a space and you now land inside it, not back on the list. One
card asks **what it's for**: Family, Club or group, Neighbours, or Just
a feed. Each choice switches on the matching sections, and you can
flip any of them on or off yourself. Skip it and the space keeps the
basics: Feed, Chat and Calendar. A one-person book club no longer
starts with nine tabs.

## Flat cards, everywhere

The tilted, taped paper look is gone from posts, in spaces and in the
household feed alike. Cards are flat and calm, like the redesigned
Friends page. The paper stays in one place where it is the point: the
Stickies board.

<details class="tech">
<summary>Under the hood</summary>

The space sections are real routes (`/spaces/{id}/calendar` and so
on), marked as the current page, so keyboard users no longer tab past
23 stops to reach them. **More** keeps a fixed order and fills from the
end of the tab row. Spaces created through the API now also start with
Feed, Chat and Calendar only; add more with
`PATCH /api/spaces/{id}` and a `features` body. Existing spaces don't
change. None of the redesign needed a database migration.

We checked every screen at 390 px wide: nothing scrolls sideways, and
every control is at least 36 px tall. The lowest text contrast in the
final design is 4.58 : 1, light or dark (WCAG AA asks for 4.5). Spaces
read cleanly right to left, and "Skip to main content" jumps within
the page again instead of going Home.

</details>

## Local communities

Some groups only make sense for the people nearby: the street's
parents, the village's food sharing, the swim crew at the local river
bath. A public space can now be a **local community**. When you create
it, or later in its settings, you pick a centre on the map, or use
your home as the centre, and a radius from 1 to 25 km. From then on,
only households whose home is inside that circle can join.

<div class="shots">
<figure class="desk"><a href="/blog/2026-10-spaces/lc-picker-desk.webp"><img src="/blog/2026-10-spaces/lc-picker-desk.webp" alt="The Choose the area dialog with a map of Zürich showing a 2 km circle, the centre's coordinates, a Use our home as the centre link, latitude and longitude fields, radius chips 1, 2, 5, 10 and 25 km with 2 km picked, and a note that only your household sees the centre and paired households see only the radius" width="1280" height="800" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption><b>Choose the area</b>A centre and a radius. Only your household sees the centre.</figcaption></figure>

**The centre stays with you.** It is usually someone's street, so it
never leaves your home. Paired households see only that the space is a
local community and its radius, "within 2 km". Local communities are
public spaces, so they are never listed on a [GFS](/docs/glossary/#gfs)
(Global Federation Server).

Your own household sees the map, and Discover tells you up front
whether your home is in a community's area, near the edge, or outside.

**Asked before anything is sent.** To join another household's local
community, your home has to show it lives in the area. Before your
request goes out, Social Home says exactly what will be sent: your
home's approximate location, to about 1 km, to that one household
only. They use it to decide and don't keep it. Say no, and nothing is
sent.

Clearly inside, and you're in (or your request goes to the admins, if
the space asks for one). Clearly outside, and the space says why
there's no Join button. **Near the edge**, nothing is decided
automatically: your request goes to an admin, who sees "Near the edge"
and decides. People who are already members stay members if the area
later moves or shrinks.

Your household's home location comes from Home Assistant. If you run
Social Home on its own, an admin can now set it under **Admin ›
Settings**.

<details class="tech">
<summary>Under the hood</summary>

The area is a centre stored to 4 decimal places (about 11 m) and a
radius of 0.5–50 km, kept only on the hosting household. It is not
part of the space's config that member households receive. Paired
households get `local_community` and `radius_km` in the directory
snapshot, and nothing else. A space can only be a local community on
the public scope; leaving it drops the area.

A join from another household carries `join_location`: your home
rounded to **2 decimal places** (about 1.1 km), added only when you
said yes. It travels inside the join's own sealed message, encrypted
home to home, so relays and the GFS can't read it. The host rounds it
again on arrival, and never stores, logs or forwards it. The host
decides for the whole ~1 km cell: every corner inside means inside;
the nearest point beyond the rim means outside; a cell that straddles
the rim always becomes a join request for the admins, even in an open
space. The only trace kept is the verdict on a pending request.

What remains: an automatic answer tells the person asking which ~1 km
cell is inside or outside, so someone patient could map the rim at
that resolution. To limit retries, each person gets 10 checks per
community per UTC day, and each other household 20, counted in a
table that survives restarts and stores people only as a hash. Local
communities can't use delegated admin authority, so every seat passes
the host's check. The full rules are in the core repo's
`docs/protocol/local-communities.md`.

</details>

## Global spaces, nearest first

A global space is for anyone, on the GFSs its owner picks. Its owner
can now pin it to a place, like a chess club in Zürich, and optionally
add a hint like "best for people within 10 km". In **Discover ›
Global**, pinned spaces are sorted by distance from your home, and
**Near me** keeps only the ones whose suggested area you're in.

The pin is only for sorting. Nobody checks where you live when you
join a global space; its About page just reminds you that you're
expected to follow the space's rules. The distances are worked out in
your own browser, and your home is never sent anywhere for it.

<details class="tech">
<summary>Under the hood</summary>

The pin (`place: {lat, lon, radius_km?}`, 4 decimal places) is part of
the global listing, so it is visible to everyone who can find the
space, including the GFS that lists it. The app says so where you set
it, and it is never pre-filled from your home. It is covered by the
owner's Ed25519 signature on the listing, so a GFS can't add, move or
strip it. Distance labels are rounded: under 1 km, the nearest km up
to 10, the nearest 5 km above.

</details>

## Tighter walls

The review also turned up problems that weren't about looks, and we
fixed those first. The most serious: someone in your household who was
never invited could read a private space's posts and member list.
That's fixed, along with 19 related gaps found in two security reviews
that followed:

- Asking about a space you can't see gets the same answer whether it
  exists or not.
- People banned from a space can no longer read it.
- Followers see a shorter member list: no online status, no locations,
  no admins' proposals, and not who else follows.
- Bazaar listings and gallery albums stay inside their space, and
  search no longer counts matches in spaces you're not in.
- Pairing never sends your home location when you've switched sharing
  off.

The new Spaces are in **Social Home 2026.10.20**. Open **Spaces**, see
