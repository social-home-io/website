Every space in Social Home lives on one household: its **host**. Between
paired households, that was never a bottleneck. Posts travel directly
from one Home Assistant to the other, or hop across the mesh of
households you both know, and the host doesn't need to be awake for
any of it.

The path through the [GFS (Global Federation Server)](/docs/glossary/#gfs)
— the relay that helps households find each other — was different.
It carries a space to households you aren't paired with: followers
of a public space, or members who joined a private space through a
link. On that path, the host had to sign and relay every post. If
the host's Home Assistant was rebooting, updating or simply offline,
the space went quiet for everyone who reached it through the GFS.

Not anymore. With **Social Home 2026.10.4**, members publish their own
posts, comments and reactions straight through the GFS, and the host
no longer has to be online. The GFS path now works the way direct
federation and the mesh always have. It took five pull requests and a
lot of protocol work, and we couldn't be happier with how it turned
out. And the GFS still never sees what anyone posts.

_Everything in this post is about the GFS path only. Direct
household-to-household federation and the mesh are unchanged._

## Members post for themselves

When a household gets write rights in a space, it now gets a small,
signed permission slip from the space along with them. From then on
it posts straight through the GFS on its own; the GFS checks the slip
and passes the sealed post on to everyone following the space. The
host is not in the loop any more.

The GFS still sees nothing it can read. It can't tell a post from a
comment or a reaction — they all arrive as sealed envelopes of the
same few sizes. Lose your write rights (removed, banned, left, or
narrowed to comments only) and your slip stops working at the same
moment. Households on older versions keep getting every post, because
the host keeps relaying as before and receivers simply drop the
duplicates.

<details class="tech">
<summary>Under the hood</summary>

The permission slip is a **writer certificate**: the space's
authority key signs `{space, key epoch, household key, scope}` with
scope `write` or `comment` — one Ed25519 signature per writer
household per epoch, never one per post. A member attaches the
certificate and publishes directly; the GFS verifies it against the
space key it has pinned, then fans the item out to followers as a
generic `space_item` plus ciphertext. Payloads are padded to fixed
size buckets (1, 4, 16, 64 and 128 KiB), so a reaction and a post
look alike. Losing write rights rotates the key epoch, which
invalidates the old certificate. Comments, reactions, edits and
deletes travel the same way and may arrive in any order: a delete
that arrives before its post still wins, and edits resolve by the
author-signed edit time.

</details>

## Trusted or strict: the owner decides

The space owner chooses what the GFS may learn about who is posting.

- **Trusted** (the default): the GFS sees which household posted,
  never what.
- **Strict**: members post anonymously. The GFS can't tell which
  household a post came from — and can't even tell which households
  are allowed to write.

<details class="tech">
<summary>Under the hood</summary>

In strict mode all writers share a per-epoch writer key derived from
the space seed. A publish carries no household id and no household
signature; the writer certificate rides inside the ciphertext.
Writers subscribe in the same shuffled batch as followers, so the
GFS can't separate writers from readers.

</details>

## Private spaces can use the GFS too

A private space normally never touches the GFS: its members are
households you're paired with, and they reach each other directly or
over the mesh. Now the owner can switch the GFS on for a private
space. It's **off by default**. When it's on, anyone with a
**GFS link** can join without pairing first, and members who are only
reachable through the GFS get each other's posts while the host is
offline.

Even then, the GFS never learns which space it is carrying — not its
name, not its key, not what's in it. It only learns which households
take part.

Nobody gets cut off by accident. While households that joined through
a GFS link are still in the space, turning the GFS off is refused, and
the settings list exactly which households to remove first. Every
invite link now shows its type: a **GFS link** for anyone, or a
**Local link** that only works for households you're already
connected to and never touches the GFS.

<details class="tech">
<summary>Under the hood</summary>

A private space with the GFS on is represented on the relay by a
random 128-bit channel id with its own key, derived with HKDF so it
can't be linked back to the space key. The GFS sees the member
households on that channel, never the space's id, name, key or
content.

</details>

## The GFS learns less than ever

While we were in there, we made the GFS see less across the board:

- **A space sync tells the GFS nothing.** Syncing a space directly
  with another household no longer involves the GFS at all, so there
  is no record of who synced when.
- **The GFS never learns who relayed.** Space events go out without
  any household identity attached.
- **Invite-link traffic is padded.** The GFS sees a rough size, never
  the exact one.
- **Outages don't lose events.** If the GFS hiccups, your household
  keeps trying. Messages to members who are briefly unreachable are
  held and sent as soon as they're back.

<details class="tech">
<summary>Under the hood</summary>

Direct syncs used to ask the GFS for a signalling node nobody used;
that call is gone. The old identified publish is removed: space
events go out as `{space_id, event_type, payload}` on a cookie-less
session with no household id. Envelopes to members who joined by
link are padded to size buckets. Publishes that hit a GFS error are
retried with backoff (5 s, 30 s, 2 min, 10 min), and the GFS queues
frames for offline recipients for up to 24 hours.

</details>

## Connect during onboarding

The last step of the welcome tour now offers to connect your household
to the Social Home GFS. The box is **unchecked**, so nothing is sent
unless you tick it. It says in plain words what the GFS lets you do
and what it can see, and the app checks that it really is the Social
Home GFS before connecting.

## Try it

All of this is in **Social Home 2026.10.4**. Older households keep
working: on the GFS path they simply fall back to the host until they
update, and direct federation is untouched either way.
