Every space in Social Home lives on one household: its **host**. Until
now, the host also had to sign and relay every post that went out
through the GFS (Global Federation Server). If the host's Home
Assistant was rebooting, updating or simply offline, the space went
quiet for everyone who reached it through the GFS.

Not anymore. With **Social Home 2026.10.4**, members publish their own
posts, comments and reactions straight through the GFS, and the host
no longer has to be online. It took five pull requests and a lot of
protocol work, and we couldn't be happier with how it turned out. And
the GFS still never sees what anyone posts.

## Writer certificates: one signature, not one per post

The core idea is the **writer certificate**. When a household gets
write rights in a space, the space's authority key signs a small
certificate for it: which space, which key epoch, which household key,
and the scope (`write` or `comment`). That's one Ed25519 signature
per writer household per epoch, never one per post.

A member now attaches that certificate and publishes directly. The GFS
checks it against the space key it has pinned, then fans the item out
to followers. What the GFS sees is a generic `space_item` and
ciphertext. It doesn't learn the content, and it doesn't even learn
whether the item is a post, a comment or a reaction. Payloads are
padded to fixed size buckets (1, 4, 16, 64 and 128 KiB) so a reaction
and a post look the same.

Losing write rights means the key epoch rotates. That covers being
removed, banned, leaving, or being narrowed from write to comment.
The old certificate then simply stops counting. And the host keeps
relaying as before, so households on older versions still get every
post. Receivers drop the duplicates.

Comments, reactions, edits and deletes travel the same way. They can
arrive in any order: a delete that arrives before its post still wins,
and edits resolve by the author-signed edit time.

## Trusted or strict: the owner decides

The space owner chooses what the GFS may learn.

- **Trusted** (the default): the GFS sees which household posted,
  never what.
- **Strict**: members post anonymously. All writers share a per-epoch
  writer key derived from the space seed. A publish carries no
  household id and no household signature, and the certificate rides
  inside the ciphertext. Writers subscribe in the same shuffled batch
  as followers, so the GFS can't even tell who may write.

## Private spaces get an opaque channel

Private spaces can use the GFS too, if the owner wants that. It's
**off by default** for new private spaces. When it's on, anyone with a
**GFS link** can join without pairing first, and members get each
other's posts while the host is offline.

The GFS never learns which space it is carrying. It sees a random
128-bit channel id with its own key, derived so it can't be linked to
the space key. It learns which member households take part, never the
space's id, name, key or content.

Nobody gets cut off by accident. While households that joined through
a GFS link are still in the space, turning the GFS off is refused, and
the settings list exactly which households to remove first. Every
invite link now shows its type: a **GFS link** for anyone, or a
**Local link** that only works for households you're already
connected to and never touches the GFS.

## The GFS learns less than ever

While we were in there, we made the GFS see less across the board:

- **A space sync tells the GFS nothing.** Direct syncs used to ask the
  GFS for a signaling node that nobody actually used. That call is
  gone, and with it the record of who synced when.
- **The GFS never learns who relayed.** The old identified publish is
  removed. Space events go out on a cookie-less session with no
  household id.
- **Invite-link traffic is padded.** Envelopes to members who joined
  by link are padded to size buckets, so the GFS sees a bucket, never
  the exact size.
- **Outages don't lose events.** Publishes that hit a GFS hiccup are
  retried with backoff. Messages to members who are briefly
  unreachable are queued and sent as soon as they're back.

## Connect during onboarding

The last step of the welcome tour now offers to connect your household
to the Social Home GFS. The box is **unchecked**, so nothing is sent
unless you tick it. It says in plain words what the GFS lets you do
and what it can see, and the app checks that it really is the Social
Home GFS before connecting.

## Try it

All of this is in **Social Home 2026.10.4**. Older households keep
working: they simply fall back to the host path until they update.
